How this is calculated
  1. Arm-load split. In a two-arm pull-up, each arm shares the total load moved (bodyweight + added weight): per-arm load = (bodyweight + added weight) / 2. In a one-arm pull-up, one arm carries the full load alone. Converting between them means doubling or halving that per-arm number.
  2. Reps → %1RM. %1RM = 80 + 20 × 2^((1 - reps) / 2) — a curve solved to pass exactly through 1 rep/100%, 3 reps/90%, and 5 reps/85%, flattening out for higher reps.
  3. Estimate. Your total load is divided by that percentage to estimate a one-rep max in the arm-count you entered, then converted to a two-arm number using the split above.
  1. This assumes the set entered was close to maximal effort for that rep count. Submaximal sets will overstate the result.
